*[Flowchart: escalation of feeding issues in neonatal care]*

**Feeding Issues:**
- new onset of emesis, bilious emesis, or higher frequency emesis; enteral feeds consistently backed up in feeding tube
- distended abdomen, visible bowel loops, abdominal girth > 2cm increase, abdominal firmness
- bloody stools or consistently bloody stools
- check stooling history and consistent stooling pattern

**Other clinical signs:**
- low output or frank blood in stool/urine
- temperature instability
- apnea/bradycardia/desaturation events (new onset, higher frequency, or severity)
- perfusion issues (cap refill > 3 sec), cool/mottled extremities
- metabolic acidosis or increased lactate
- new or increased respiratory support
- changes in NIRS

**Pneumatosis:**
- Hold feeds*
- Obtain KUB, Notify NICU attending

**Nonspecific findings:**
- Increased abdominal girth
- Thickened bowel wall

**Normal:**
- Provider to evaluate
- Re-initiate feeds when other clinical signs have resolved

**Troubleshoot with the following:**
- Check feeding tube placement
- Vent feeding tube/pull air out
- Renal issues
- Pre-feed position, left side down
- Glycerin mission in 24 hrs
- Consider narcotic/pain medication or use alternatives

**Hold feeds* 24 hrs, then evaluate. If reassuring, re-initiate same feeds and continue to monitor for other clinical signs.**

**NPO or trophic feeds for 24 hrs following clear KUB:**
- Reassess daily per clinical pathway
- Feed cautiously when other clinical signs have resolved

**When ready to restart feeds, see clinical pathway for re-initiation of enteral feeds.**

*Consider trophic feeding 10–20 mL/kg instead of NPO for gut priming

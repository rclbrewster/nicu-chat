*[Flowchart: escalation of feeding issues in neonatal care]*

**Feeding Issues:**
- new onset of emesis, bilious emesis, or higher frequency emesis; enteral feeds consistently backed up in feeding tube
- abdominal distension (abdomen firmer, increased girth (>2cm increase), visible bowel loops, absent or disorganized bowel sounds)
- Check stooling history for consistent stooling pattern

**Other clinical signs:**
- low output/oliguria
- hypotension requiring vasoactive medications
- temperature instability
- apnea/bradycardia/desaturation events (new onset, higher frequency, or increased severity)
- lethargy/irritability
- poor perfusion (cap refill >3 sec), cool/mottled extremities
- metabolic acidosis or increased lactate
- new or increased respiratory support
- changes in NIRS

**Normal:**
- Provider to evaluate
- Re-initiate feeds when other clinical signs have resolved

**Nonspecific findings:**
- Increased abdominal girth
- Mild ileus
- Mild thickened bowel wall

**Pneumatosis:**
- Hold feeds
- Obtain KUB
- Notify NICU attending

**Hold feeds:**
- Obtain KUB
- Notify NICU attending

**Troubleshoot with the following:**
- Check feeding tube placement
- Vent feeding tube/pull air out
- Renal consult
- Pre-feed position, left side down
- Glycerin mission in 24 hrs
- Consider narcotic/pain medication or use alternatives

**Hold feeds > 24 hrs:**
- Evaluate, if reassuring, re-initiate same feeds and continue to monitor for other clinical signs

**NPO or trophic feeds for 24 hrs:**
- Follow-up daily KUBs
- Reassess every 24 hrs
- Re-initiate feeds per feeding pathway when other clinical signs have resolved

**When ready to restart feeds:**
- Re-initiate feeds per feeding pathway when other clinical signs have resolved

*Consider trophic feeding 10–20 mL/kg instead of NPO for gut priming*
